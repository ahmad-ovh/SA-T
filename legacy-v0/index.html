DOSSIER 01

ORIGIN AND INTERIOR CONTEXT: THERMODYNAMIC RHEOLOGY

GEOMECHANICAL AUDIT

PLANETARY DIFFERENTIATION & THE HEAT ENGINE

Earth formed ~4.56 Ga via solar nebula condensation and planetesimal accretion. Gravitational potential energy release (~2.5×10³⁰ J) and radiogenic decay (²⁶Al, ²³⁸U, ²³⁵U, ²³²Th, ⁴⁰K) caused extensive melting. Dense Fe-Ni sank to form the core, while buoyant silicates formed the mantle and primitive crust (Turcotte & Schubert, 2014).

This established a thermodynamic heat engine: core-mantle heat flux drives mantle convection, converting thermal energy into mechanical plate motion.

RHEOLOGICAL REGIMES: LITHOSPHERE VS. ASTHENOSPHERE

Mechanical stratification is dictated by homologous temperature (T/T_melt) and strain rate:

  • Brittle Lithosphere (0–100 km): Cold thermal boundary (T < 1300°C). Behaves elastically, failing by Byerlee's law frictional sliding (τ = μ·σ_n) and stick-slip ruptures in the upper 0–15 km crust.
  • Ductile Asthenosphere (100–410 km): Near-solidus peridotite (T/T_melt ≈ 0.85–0.95, viscosity η ≈ 10¹⁹–10²¹ Pa·s). Deforms plastically via dislocation creep, mechanically decoupling plates.

DOSSIER 02

TECTONIC SETTING: FORCE BALANCES & DRIVING MECHANISMS

VECTOR MECHANICS REPORT

REGIONAL KINEMATIC SETTING

The San Andreas Fault System (SAFS) is a ~1,200 km continental right-lateral (dextral) strike-slip transform boundary accommodating ~48–50 mm/yr of relative motion between the Pacific Plate and North American Plate.

It connects the Mendocino Triple Junction (Pacific–North America–Gorda) in the north to the extensional Salton Trough and Gulf of California spreading rift in the south (Atwater, 1970).

RIGOROUS FORCE BALANCE JUSTIFICATION

Transform boundaries possess neither local subducting slabs nor spreading ridges. Kinematics are governed by the Pacific Plate's integrated torque balance:

  • Dominant Driver (Slab Pull): Accounts for ~90% of global driving torque (Forsyth & Uyeda, 1975). Cold, dense oceanic lithosphere sinking into Western Pacific trenches (Mariana, Kuril, Aleutian) pulls the Pacific Plate northwestward.
  • Ridge Push (F_RP): Secondarily contributes ~2–3×10¹² N/m from elevated East Pacific Rise spreading centers.
  • Resisting Force: The SAF operates as a frictional shear zone resisting motion (τ ≈ 10–30 MPa) until co-seismic slip.

THE EVIDENCE LAYER: EMPIRICAL MULTI-DATA INTEGRATION

EMPIRICAL DATA ARCHIVE

SAVAGE & BURFORD (1973) DISLOCATION MODEL

Continuous PBO GPS velocities fit an elastic screw dislocation:

v(x) = (V₀ / π) · arctan(x / D)

where V₀ ≈ 36 mm/yr (fault slip rate) and D ≈ 14 km (locking depth). Interseismic strain accumulates elastically across a 60-km aperture.

RESIDUAL RMSE: 0.84 mm/yr

MICROSEISMIC DEPTH CUTOFF

Relocated earthquake hypocentres from USGS NCEDC catalogs show seismicity abruptly terminates at ~15 km depth.

This boundary marks the 350°C Brittle-Ductile Transition Zone. Below 15 km, quartz deforms by ductile dislocation creep without generating stick-slip earthquakes.

SEAFLOOR MAGNETIC ANOMALY ISOCHRONS

Oceanic basalt magnetic striping (Chrons 1–21) recorded Pacific-Farallon seafloor spreading (Atwater, 1970).

Truncation of anomaly stripes against North America dates ridge subduction at ~30 Ma, initiating the San Andreas transform fault and migrating Mendocino/Rivera triple junctions.

GEOMORPHIC OFFSET: WALLACE CREEK

At Wallace Creek (Carrizo Plain), the incised active stream channel is offset 130 m right-laterally.

Radiocarbon dating (¹⁴C) of alluvial charcoal dates channel formation to 3,700 ± 150 yr BP (Sieh & Jahns, 1984):

DOSSIER 05

WHY IT MATTERS: HAZARDS, BASINS & MALAYSIA NEXUS

GEOTECHNICAL NEXUS

SAN ANDREAS HAZARDS & BASIN RESOURCES

Seismic Hazard: The locked Southern SAF has not ruptured since 1690 (>330 yr deficit, ~5 m slip deficit), posing an Mw 7.8+ threat. Lifelines require flexible slip joints.

Resources: Pull-apart basins (LA, Ventura) trap hydrocarbon reservoirs. Salton Sea geothermal fields yield clean energy and commercial lithium.

EXPLICIT GEODYNAMIC NEXUS: MALAYSIA

Malaysia sits on the intraplate Sunda Plate, exhibiting critical geodynamic vulnerability:

  • Sumatran Strike-Slip Analogue: The 1,900 km dextral Great Sumatran Fault shares identical kinematics with the SAF, radiating long-period waves into Peninsular Malaysia.
  • Reactivated Intraplate Faults: Ancient shear sutures (e.g., Bukit Tinggi Fault Zone) slip under compressive stress (Mw 1.5–3.5 swarms; Shuib, 2009).
  • Sabah Active Faulting: The 2015 Ranau Mw 6.0 earthquake on the Mensaban fault killed 18 on Mount Kinabalu (Tongkul, 2016).
  • Structural Codes: Malaysia Annex to Eurocode 8 (MS EN 1998-1:2015) mandates design against resonant shaking on soft clays.
